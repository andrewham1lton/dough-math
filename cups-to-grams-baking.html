Cups to grams

There's no single number of grams in a cup. A cup measures space and a gram measures weight, so the answer depends on what fills the cup.

A cup of all-purpose flour weighs about 120 g. A cup of granulated sugar weighs 200 g, and a cup of honey 336 g.

The conversion

  • Grams = cups × the ingredient's grams per cup
  • Cups = grams ÷ the ingredient's grams per cup

2½ cups of all-purpose flour is 2.5 × 120 = 300 g. 450 g of bread flour is 450 ÷ 127 = 3.54 cups, a little over 3½. Every ingredient's figure is in the grams per cup table.

Ounces and fluid ounces

An ounce on a scale is a weight. A fluid ounce on a measuring jug is a volume. They only line up for water and things like it: 8 fl oz of water weighs about 8.4 oz (237 g). 8 fl oz of flour, one cup, weighs about 4.2 oz. When a recipe gives flour in ounces, it means weight.

Milliliters to grams

Milliliters are volume too. A milliliter of water weighs about a gram. Milk and cream weigh a little more, and oil a little less. For anything else, grams = ml ÷ 236.6 × grams per cup.

Why weighing is steadier

Two people filling a cup with flour can land 20 g apart or more, from scooping or spooning, sifting, and how settled the flour is. A scale gives the same answer every time. If you bake by the cup, weigh your own cup once and use that figure.
