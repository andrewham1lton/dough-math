Writing a cake quote

A quote tells the customer what they'll get, when, and what it costs, before they commit. Written down, it answers most questions before they're asked.

What to put on it

  • Your bakery's name and how to reach you
  • The customer's name and contact
  • The date of the quote
  • The date the order is wanted, and pickup or delivery
  • Each item as you'll make it: size, tiers, servings, flavor, filling, frosting, colors and design
  • The price of each item
  • Extras on their own line: delivery, setup, a rush order
  • The total, any deposit, and the balance left to pay
  • When the deposit and the balance are due, and how you take payment
  • How long the quote holds, since ingredient prices move

If you're unsure whether anything else belongs on it where you live, check your local rules.

What to leave off

Your costs and your margin. The quote is what the customer pays, not how you got there. Work the price out first, with how to price a cake, then write the quote from the result.

Changes and cancellations

Say in a sentence or two what happens if the date, the design or the servings change after the quote, and what happens to the deposit if the order is cancelled. Plain words you'd say out loud work well. What you decide is up to you.

Send it before you bake

A quote comes before the work. Send it as a PDF the customer can keep, and wait for a yes, and the deposit if you take one, before you buy for the order.
