The week's shopping list

With several orders in a week, shopping recipe by recipe means extra trips, or a run on flour late on a Thursday. Add up the week first, then buy whole packs.

How to build it

  1. List the orders confirmed for the week. Leave out inquiries until they're a yes.
  2. For each order, multiply each recipe's ingredients by the number of batches.
  3. Add up each ingredient across all the orders. Weight is easiest: turn cups into grams with each ingredient's grams per cup.
  4. Divide by the size of the pack you buy, and round up to whole packs.
  5. Take off what's already in the cupboard.

A worked example

Say the week's three confirmed orders need 6,300 g of bread flour, 1,135 g of butter and 30 eggs between them.

Bread flour, 3 bags
6,300 g is 2.8 bags of 5 lb, at $5.49 a bag$16.47

Butter, 3 packs
1,135 g is 2.5 packs of 1 lb, at $4.99 a pack$14.97

Eggs, 3 dozen
30 eggs is 2.5 dozen, at $3.60 a dozen$10.80

To buy$42.24

What you buy and what you use

The bakes themselves use $15.25 of the flour, $12.49 of the butter and $9.00 of the eggs: $36.74. The other $5.50 stays in the cupboard for next week.

That's why a recipe's cost counts only what the recipe uses, while the list counts what you pay at the till. Both are right, for different questions. How a recipe's cost is worked out.

Rounding up, and when not to

Rounding up means you never run short halfway through a bake. 2.1 bags rounds up to 3 as well, and leaves most of a bag over. If the extra is small and you have some at home, buying one fewer is your call.
