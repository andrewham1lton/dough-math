What one recipe costs

You buy flour by the bag and bake by the cup, so the price on the receipt never matches the amount in the recipe. Three numbers get you from one to the other: what you paid, how much was in the pack, and how much the recipe uses.

The rule

Line cost = price paid × amount used ÷ amount in the pack.

The amount used and the amount in the pack have to be in the same unit. That's the whole method. The work is in getting the units to match.

From a bag of flour to a cup

A 5 lb bag of all-purpose flour costs $5.49.

  • A pound is 453.59 g, so the bag holds 2,268 g.
  • A cup of all-purpose flour weighs about 120 g, so the bag holds about 18.9 cups.
  • A cup costs $5.49 × 120 ÷ 2,268, about 29 cents.
  • Three cups (360 g) cost $5.49 × 360 ÷ 2,268, about 87 cents.

The weight of a cup is what joins the two. Without it, a pound and a cup can't be compared. Grams per cup for common ingredients.

When the units already match

Some lines need no weight at all. Eggs bought by the dozen and used one at a time: a dozen at $3.60 is 30 cents an egg. Vanilla bought by the fluid ounce and used by the teaspoon: both are volumes, and a fluid ounce is 6 teaspoons. Butter bought by the pound and used by the stick: a stick is a quarter pound, so a quarter of the price.

Habits that keep it right

  • Round each line to the cent, then add the lines. The list then always adds up to its total.
  • Use the price you last paid, and change it when it changes. A recipe costed on last year's butter is out of date.
  • Watch the big lines more than the small ones. In this batch the chocolate chips and butter are two thirds of the cost, and the salt is a cent.
