Overhead and packaging

Two costs sit outside the recipe. Packaging leaves the kitchen with the bake. Overhead keeps the kitchen running and belongs to no single bake.

Packaging: count it every time

Packaging goes out the door with the order, so count it at what it cost you, on every bake. A box of a dozen cookies might be:

Bakery box$1.10

Liner$0.15

Ribbon$0.30

Sticker with your name$0.10

Packaging$1.65

Bought by the case? Divide the price by the count. A case of 50 boxes at $55.00 is $1.10 a box.

Overhead: turn it into a percentage

Overhead is real money but hard to pin to one cake: power for the oven, pans and mixers that wear out, parchment and foil, card reader fees, a market stall. A simple way to carry it:

  1. Add up a typical month's overhead.
  2. Add up the same month's ingredients and labor.
  3. Divide the first by the second.

Oven power, your share$25.00

Pans, tools and mixer wear$15.00

Parchment, foil and wrap$12.00

Card reader fees$14.00

Market stall$26.00

Overhead for the month$92.00

If the same month's ingredients and labor came to $920, overhead is $92 ÷ $920 = 10%. Each bake then carries 10% of its ingredients and labor as overhead.

Why the percentage skips packaging

Packaging is already counted at its own cost, so the percentage is taken on ingredients and labor only. This is how Dough Math and the calculator on this site work it out: overhead = (ingredients + labor) × overhead %.

Look at it again now and then

Overhead drifts. Work the percentage out again when something changes, like a new oven, a new fee or a busier season.

Where Dough Math fits

Each Dough Math recipe has its packaging cost, and overhead is a percentage you set in Settings.

Labor minutes at your hourly rate, packaging and overhead count toward the cost, so the suggested price covers more than the flour.
